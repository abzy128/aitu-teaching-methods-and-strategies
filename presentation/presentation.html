Theoretical Foundations
III.Theoretical Anchors

Two frameworks, one prediction.

FRAMEWORK 01
Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning
Mayer · 1990s — present
Learners process verbal and visual information through partially independent channels of limited capacity.
  • Modality  ·  Signalling
  • Coherence  ·  Redundancy
  • Personalisation  ·  Contiguity
FRAMEWORK 02
Cognitive Load Theory
Sweller and colleagues
Working memory is finite; learning depends on minimising extraneous load and channelling germane load to schema construction.
  • Intrinsic load — the material
  • Extraneous load — the packaging
  • Germane load — the learning
Convergent prediction

Substituting well-designed visual explanations for dense text should reduce extraneous load and free working memory for schema construction — especially for learners whose cognitive resources are already taxed by the language itself.

Finding 01 · Typology
V.Finding 01 — Typology

Four categories.

i.
Generated Static Visuals
Diagrams, illustrations and infographics produced by text-to-image or multimodal models from a learner or teacher prompt.
Bewersdorff et al. · 2024
ii.
Augmented & Annotated Visuals
An AI system enriches existing content with labels, callouts and signalling cues calibrated to the learner's level.
The signalling principle, operationalised
iii.
Adaptive Multimodal Sequences
Visual content embedded in a multi-step pipeline — transcription, engagement, reinforcement.
Osman et al. · 2024
iv.
AI-Mediated Augmented Reality
Personalised visual scaffolding overlaid on the physical or printed environment.
Khoirunnisa et al. · 2024

Finding 02 · Theory Alignment
VI.Finding 02 — Alignment

Where the corpus meets the theory.

Strong alignment — supported principles

Modality principle
Verbal information presented as narration alongside visuals — operationalised by Bewersdorff et al. and Osman et al.
Signalling principle
MLLM-mediated annotation can selectively emphasise elements relevant to a learner's current question.
Coherence principle
Adaptive systems prune visual content to the learner's level — implicit in most reviewed implementations.

Weak alignment — unresolved tensions

Redundancy risk
Default behaviour of generative AI — producing both image and text — risks duplicating information across channels.
Personalisation register
Conversational tone is well-served, but raises questions of register for non-native speakers.
Active processing
Lawson & Mayer (2024): drawing did not produce gains; summarising did. The visual must be paired with cognitive engagement.

Finding 04 · Implementation Barriers
VIII.Finding 04 — Barriers

Five recurring concerns.

01
Hallucination
Plausible but factually incorrect outputs — particularly hazardous for learners who lack the prior knowledge to detect errors.
02
Algorithmic bias
Models trained on Western, English, neurotypical defaults may produce exclusionary visual content.
03
Privacy
Third-party processing of learner data — diagnostic information is especially sensitive.
04
Equity of access
Subscription costs and connectivity gaps risk widening the very divide the technology claims to narrow.
05
Educator role
AI should augment, not replace, teacher judgement — particularly where pedagogical decisions are clinical.
§ 4.4 Findings
Design + safeguards, not design alone

Conclusion · Future Directions
IX.Conclusion

AI-generated visual explanations are neither a panacea nor a passing trend. They are a capability whose pedagogical potential is real, partially evidenced, and conditional on careful design.

DIRECTION 01
Longitudinal studies of sustained exposure
DIRECTION 02
Differential effects across disability profiles
DIRECTION 03
Controlled tests of adaptive load management
DIRECTION 04
Culturally responsive design — including Kazakhstani contexts
